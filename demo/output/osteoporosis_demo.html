Domain definition
-----------------

Conditions:
- anorexia nervosa
description: Severe eating disorder characterized by self-imposed starvation, profound weight loss, and early-onset bone mineral loss.
mechanisms: profound estrogen deficiency from hypothalamic amenorrhea, nutritional depletion of calcium, nutritional depletion of vitamin D, nutritional depletion of protein, elevated baseline cortisol and low anabolic hormones IGF 1
signals: extremely low body mass index BMI, primary or secondary amenorrhea, low serum IGF 1 and leptin levels
interventions: nutritional rehabilitation and weight restoration, restoration of menstrual function transdermal estrogen if needed, calcium and vitamin D supplementation, Cognitive behavioral therapy CBT


- celiac disease
description: Immune-mediated enteropathy triggered by dietary gluten ingestion leading to small intestinal mucosal damage and malabsorption.
mechanisms: malabsorption of calcium and fat soluble vitamin D, secondary hyperparathyroidism, impaired bone mineralization
signals: positive anti tTG IgA antibodies, gastrointestinal distress, low serum vitamin D and calcium, elevated alkaline phosphatase
interventions: strict lifelong gluten free diet, high dose vitamin D and calcium supplementation, routine bone density monitoring


- CKD MBD
description: Chronic Kidney Disease-Mineral and Bone Disorder (CKD-MBD) is a systemic disorder of mineral and bone metabolism due to chronic kidney disease manifesting as biochemical and bone abnormalities.
mechanisms: phosphate retention stimulating FGF23 and secondary hyperparathyroidism, skeletal resistance to PTH resulting in altered bone turnover states
signals: elevated serum fibroblast growth factor 23 FGF23, hyperphosphatemia and abnormal intact PTH levels, vascular calcifications on diagnostic imaging
interventions: dietary phosphate restriction, phosphate binders, sevelamer, calcium acetate, calcimimetics cinacalcet, active vitamin D analogs


- diabetes mellitus
description: Chronic metabolic disorders characterized by hyperglycemia that negatively impact bone material properties and structural integrity over time.
mechanisms: accumulation of advanced glycation end products AGEs impairing collagen cross linking, chronic microvascular disease reducing bone perfusion, direct inhibitory effects of high glucose on osteoblast differentiation
signals: fractures occurring despite normal or mildly reduced BMD T scores, impaired fracture healing rates, chronically elevated glycated hemoglobin
interventions: strict glycemic control using bone neutral agents, avoidance of anti diabetic medications known to increase fracture risk, comprehensive fall prevention strategies


- glucocorticoid induced osteoporosis
description: Glucocorticoid-Induced Osteoporosis (GIOP) is scondary osteoporosis caused by systemic administration of exogenous glucocorticoids for inflammatory or autoimmune conditions.
mechanisms: inhibition of osteoblast differentiation, promotion of osteocyte apoptosis, increased urinary calcium excretion, secondary inhibition of intestinal calcium absorption
signals: rapid bone loss shortly after therapy initiation, fragility fractures at higher BMD thresholds, back pain from vertebral compression
interventions: dose minimization or discontinuation, bisphosphonates, teriparatide, calcium and vitamin D supplementation


- hyperparathyroidism
description: Condition characterized by excessive secretion of parathyroid hormone (PTH) leading to abnormal calcium and phosphorus metabolism.
mechanisms: cortical bone loss, high PTH levels stimulating bone resorption
signals: hypercalcemia, hypophosphatemia, elevated serum intact PTH, subperiosteal bone resorption on radiographs
interventions: surgical parathyroidectomy, vitamin D repletion, cinacalcet, anti resorptive agents


- hyperthyroidism
description: Condition of excessive thyroid hormone production that accelerates overall systemic metabolic and bone turnover rates.
mechanisms: excess triiodothyronine T3 directly stimulates osteoclast activity, shortening of the bone remodeling cycle where resorption outpaces formation
signals: suppressed serum TSH with elevated free T3 and T4, unexplained weight loss and tachycardia, hypercalciuria, mild hypercalcemia
interventions: anti thyroid medications, methimazole, radioactive iodine ablation, surgical thyroidectomy, calcium and vitamin D supplementation


- postmenopausal osteoporosis
description: Postmenopausal osteoporosis is the most common form of osteoporosis, occurring in women following the cessation of ovarian function and estrogen production
mechanisms: estrogen deficiency, enhanced osteoclast formation and activity, reduced osteoblast lifespan
signals: low BMD t score, asymptomatic height loss, fragility, fractures
interventions: bisphosphonates, Hormone replacement therapy, calcium and vitamin D supplementation, denosumab


- primary biliary cholangitis
description: Primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) is a chronic progressive autoimmune liver disease characterized by destruction of small intrahepatic bile ducts leading to cholestatic metabolic bone disease.
mechanisms: impaired bile flow leading to severe malabsorption of fat soluble vitamins D and K, direct inhibitory effects of chronic cholestasis and cytokines on osteoblasts
signals: positive anti mitochondrial antibodies AMA, elevated serum alkaline phosphatase and gamma glutamyl transferase GGT, pruritus, fatigue and low BMD on screening
interventions: ursodeoxycholic acid UDCA therapy, high dose fat soluble vitamin supplementation, bisphosphonate therapy, evaluation for liver transplantation in advanced disease


- rheumatoid arthritis
description: Rheumatoid arthritiss is Chronic systemic inflammatory autoimmune disease attacking synovial joints and inducing systemic bone loss.
mechanisms: upregulation of pro inflammatory cytokines, activation of the RANKL pathway promoting osteoclastogenesis
signals: elevated inflammatory markers, joint swelling and pain, generalized reduction in bone density
interventions: DMARDs and biologic agents, concurrent bisphosphonate therapy, physical therapy


Treatment Regimens:
- bone forming anabolic therapy
description: A high-potency regimen utilizing parathyroid hormone analogs to preferentially stimulate osteoblastic bone formation over resorption, reserved for patients with severe osteoporosis or very high fracture risk.
interventions: teriparatide, romosozumab
intended effects: rapidly increase trabecular and cortical bone mass, improve bone microarchitecture and structural strength, reduce vertebral and clinical fracture risk
risks: none


- first line antiresorptive therapy
description: First-Line Antiresorptive Therapy (Bisphosphonates) is a foundational pharmacological approach using nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates to inhibit osteoclast-mediated bone resorption, commonly prescribed for postmenopausal women and men with primary osteoporosis.
interventions: alendronate, zoledronic acid, calcium, vitamin d3
intended effects: increase bone mineral density BMD for spine hip and femoral neck, reduce fracture risk, suppress bone turnover markers
risks: none


- sclerostin inhibition therapy
description: A targeted biologic approach that both increases bone formation and decreases bone resorption by inhibiting sclerostin, a regulatory protein produced by osteocytes.
interventions: romoszumab, sequential alendronate, sequential denosumab
intended effects: promote bone formation, inhibit bone resorption, increase spinal and hip bone mineral density BMD, reduce fracture risk
risks: none


Interventions:
- antioxidant dietary therapy
description: Antioxidant dietary factors and supplementation therapy represent an important integrative and supportive approach in the holistic management of osteoporosis. Oxidative stress_driven by an excess of reactive oxygen species (ROS) plays a critical pathological role in bone remodeling by upregulating osteoclastogenesis and promoting osteoblast apoptosis, ultimately leading to accelerated bone loss.
indicated agents: green tea extract, vitamin E, vitamin C


- antiresorptive therapy
description: Bisphosphonates are a class of medications widely used to prevent and treat osteoporosis by slowing down the bone resorption process. They work by binding to hydroxyapatite crystals in bone, inhibiting osteoclast activity, which helps preserve bone density and reduce fracture risk.
indicated agents: zoledronic acid, alendronate, denosumab


- bisphosphonate therapy
description: Bisphosphonates are a class of medications widely used to prevent and treat osteoporosis by slowing down the bone resorption process. They work by binding to hydroxyapatite crystals in bone, inhibiting osteoclast activity, which helps preserve bone density and reduce fracture risk.
indicated agents: zoledronic acid, ibandronate, risedronate, alendronate


- calcitonin
description: Calcitonin is a peptide hormone naturally produced by the parafollicular cells (C-cells) of the thyroid gland. In the context of osteoporosis, synthetic salmon calcitonin is typically used as a pharmacological intervention. It exerts its primary therapeutic effect by directly binding to receptors on osteoclasts, thereby inhibiting their resorptive activity and reducing bone turnover.
indicated agents: zoledronic acid, calcium and vitamin D supplementation, denosumab, alendronate


- calcium and vitamin D supplementation
description: Achieving and maintaining an adequate vitamin D and calcium status is the foundational cornerstone of prevention and management for all patients with osteoporosis. Calcium is the essential mineral constituent required for the mineralization of the organic bone matrix (osteoid), while vitamin D is critically required for the active intestinal absorption of calcium and phosphorus, as well as for proper neuromuscular function and bone mineralization. Insufficient intake or deficiency leads to secondary hyperparathyroidism, accelerated bone resorption, loss of bone mineral density, and heightened skeletal fragility.
indicated agents: calcium citrate, calcium carbonate, vitamin D3


- denosumab
description: Denosumab is a fully human monoclonal antibody that effectively inhibits osteoclast formation, function, and survival, leading to a significant decrease in bone resorption, increased bone mineral density (BMD), and a reduced risk of vertebral, non-vertebral, and hip fractures in patients with osteoporosis.
indicated agents: zoledronic acid, calcium and vitamin D supplementation, teriparatide therapy, denosumab, alendronate


- dkk1 neutralizing antibodies
description: dkk1 neutralizing antibodies are biologic interventions utilizing monoclonal antibodies directed against Dickkopf-1 (DKK1), an endogenous inhibitor of the Wnt/beta-catenin signaling pathway. By blocking DKK1, these agents promote osteoblast differentiation, enhance bone formation, and reverse age- or disease-related trabecular and cortical bone deterioration.
indicated agents: BHQ880, IDs 03


- estrogen receptor agonism
description: Pharmacological therapies utilizing estrogen receptor agonists or selective estrogen receptor modulators (SERMs) to activate estrogen pathways in skeletal tissue, inhibiting osteoclast-mediated bone resorption, preventing postmenopausal bone loss, and reducing the incidence of vertebral fractures.
indicated agents: bazedoxifene conjugated estrogens, raloxifene hydrochloride


- estrogen therapy
description: A targeted hormonal treatment utilizing estrogens (often combined with a progestin in women with an intact uterus) to compensate for postmenopausal decline in endogenous estrogen, aiming to restore bone remodeling equilibrium, preserve bone mineral density, and prevent fragility fractures.
indicated agents: transdermal estradiol patch, calcium and vitamin D supplementation, progesterone


- glycemic control
description: Glycemic control is an increasingly recognized and vital metabolic intervention in the comprehensive management of patients with osteoporosis, particularly those with concurrent type 2 diabetes mellitus. Chronic hyperglycemia impairs bone quality and strength through the accumulation of advanced glycation end-products (AGEs) in the organic bone matrix, increases oxidative stress, alters osteoblast differentiation, and negatively impacts bone material properties independent of changes in bone mineral density (BMD)
indicated agents: calcium and vitamin D supplementation, GLP 1 receptor agonists, SGLT2 inhibitors, metformin


- gsk3 beta inhibitors
description: GSK3_beta_inhibitors are small molecule therapeutic agents that inhibit glycogen synthase kinase-3 beta (GSK-3beta), thereby stabilizing beta-catenin and upregulating Wnt signaling pathway activity to stimulate osteoblastic bone formation and mitigate bone mineral density decline.
indicated agents: SB 216763, tideglusib


- hormone replacement therapy
description: Hormone replacement therapy (HRT) is a systemic endocrine intervention utilizing estrogen alone (for women without a uterus) or combined with a progestogen (for women with an intact uterus) to suppress bone turnover, prevent bone mineral density loss, and alleviate menopausal symptoms.
indicated agents: conjugated equine etrogens with medroxyprogesterone acetate, conjugated equine etrogens, 17-Beta Estradiol


- mechanical loading stimulation
description: Mechanical loading and targeted exercise therapy represent fundamental non-pharmacological interventions for the prevention and management of osteoporosis.
indicated agents: physical therapy supervision rehabilitation, transdermal estradiol patch, calcium and vitamin D supplementation, alendronate


- multinucleated resorbing cells
description: A primary antiresorptive therapeutic strategy focused on suppressing the activity, differentiation, and survival of multinucleated osteoclasts to halt excessive bone turnover, prevent trabecular and cortical bone loss, and reduce fracture risk in osteoporotic patients.
indicated agents: zoledronic acid, denosumab, alendronate


- parathyroid hormone pth anabolic regimens
description: Anabolic pharmacological regimens utilizing intermittent administration of parathyroid hormone or related peptides to stimulate osteoblastic bone formation, significantly improving bone mineral density, microarchitecture, and overall skeletal strength in patients with severe osteoporosis.
indicated agents: abaloparatide, teriparatide


- raloxifene
description: Raloxifene** is a selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM) designed to mimic the beneficial estrogenic effects on bone mineral density and bone turnover while acting as an antagonist in uterine and breast tissues. By binding to estrogen receptors, raloxifene exerts an anti-resorptive effect on the skeleton, reducing bone resorption and slowing bone loss, which significantly lowers the risk of vertebral fractures in postmenopausal women with osteoporosis.
indicated agents: raloxifene hydrochloride


- recombinant osteoprotegerin
description: Recombinant osteoprotegerin is a biological antiresorptive intervention utilizing recombinant forms of the decoy receptor osteoprotegerin to bind and neutralize RANKL, thereby suppressing osteoclastogenesis, reducing bone resorption, and preserving bone mineral density in postmenopausal or secondary osteoporosis.
indicated agents: recombinant human osteoprotegerin


- reduction of oxidative stressors
description: The reduction of oxidative stressors is an emerging and vital therapeutic paradigm in the management of osteoporosis. Oxidative stress—characterized by an imbalance between the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and antioxidant defense mechanisms—plays a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of bone loss.
indicated agents: resveratrol, vitamin E, vitamin C


- regular weight bearing exercise
description: Mechanical loading and targeted exercise therapy represent fundamental non-pharmacological interventions for the prevention and management of osteoporosis.
indicated agents: physical therapy supervision rehabilitation, transdermal estradiol patch, calcium and vitamin D supplementation, alendronate


- romosozumab
description: Romosozumab is an advanced bone-forming (anabolic) therapeutic intervention utilizing a humanized monoclonal antibody against sclerostin to stimulate new bone formation and simultaneously decrease bone resorption, providing rapid gains in bone mineral density and fracture risk reduction.
indicated agents: romosozumab


- selective estrogen receptor modulators
description: Selective Estrogen Receptor Modulators (SERMs) are a pharmacological class of compounds that exert estrogenic agonist effects on bone tissue to inhibit bone resorption and increase bone mineral density, while exhibiting antagonist properties in breast and uterine tissues, utilized primarily for the prevention and treatment of postmenopausal osteoporosis.
indicated agents: bazedoxifene conjugated estrogens, raloxifene hydrochloride


- senolytics dasatinib quercetin
description: Senolytics (Dasatinib + Quercetin) is a combination senolytic therapy utilizing the tyrosine kinase inhibitor dasatinib and the natural flavonoid quercetin to selectively clear senescent cells from bone and bone marrow tissues. By eliminating senescent cells and reducing the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP), this intervention mitigates local inflammation, preserves osteoblast function, and ameliorates age-related trabecular bone loss.
indicated agents: fisetin augmented senolytic protocol, dasatinib quercetin combination regimen


- strontium ranelate modulation
description: A dual-action therapeutic intervention that re-couples bone remodeling by stimulating osteoblast-mediated bone formation and inhibiting osteoclast-mediated bone resorption, leading to improved bone mineral density and enhanced structural strength.
indicated agents: strontium ranelate


- teriparatide pth analog intermittent
description: Teriparatide is a recombinant human parathyroid hormone (specifically amino acids 1 to 34, rhPTH[1-34]) administered via intermittent daily subcutaneous injections. Unlike continuous parathyroid hormone elevation (which promotes bone resorption), intermittent administration of teriparatide exerts a net anabolic (bone-building) effect.
indicated agents: zoledronic acid, calcium and vitamin D supplementation, teriparatide therapy, alendronate


- teriparatide therapy
description: Teriparatide is a recombinant human parathyroid hormone (specifically amino acids 1 to 34, rhPTH[1-34]) administered via intermittent daily subcutaneous injections. - tgf beta signaling
description: Therapeutic interventions targeting the Transforming Growth Factor-Beta (TGF-beta) signaling pathway to enhance coupling between bone resorption and formation, stimulating osteoblast differentiation and recruitment while improving bone matrix mineralization in osteoporotic states.
indicated agents: TGF beta receptor type I kinase inhibitor, rh TGF beta1


- wnt signaling activators
description: Wnt signaling activators are endogenous proteins or exogenous compounds that stimulate the canonical Wnt/β-catenin pathway, which is the primary regulator of bone homeostasis. Their definition centers on their ability to promote osteoblast differentiation and bone formation while simultaneously suppressing osteoclastogenesis and bone resorption.
indicated agents: calcium and vitamin D supplementation, denosumab, alendronate, romosozumab
